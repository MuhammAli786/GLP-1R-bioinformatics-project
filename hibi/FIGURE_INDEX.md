### Pathway-level comparison of the two conditions

| File (in `figures/`) | Content | Generated by |
|---|---|---|
| `Fig13_Pathway_Overlap` | A: term overlap per database with hypergeometric enrichment. B: pathway direction scores in each arm. C: most significant shared pathways. D: gene-membership Jaccard across shared pathways. | `code/01_meta_analysis_pipeline/15_pathway_overlap_analysis.py` |

Tables: `data/meta_analysis/pathway_overlap_full.csv` (all 85 shared pathways with
per-arm direction scores, member counts and Jaccard) and
`pathway_arm_specific.csv` (top 400 arm-unique pathways per arm).

This goes beyond `Fig12C`, which only showed *which* terms were significant in
both. Here each shared pathway also gets a **direction score per arm** — the mean
log2FC of its member genes within that arm's consensus — which is a more robust
directional test than the gene-level one, because averaging over a pathway's
members suppresses the per-gene noise documented in `Fig12F`.

**Overlap.** 85 shared significant terms, 1.88× the chance expectation
(*p* = 1.4e-9). Reactome drives it (34 shared, 2.05×, *p* = 1.3e-5); GO-BP
contributes almost nothing beyond chance (10 shared, 1.08×, *p* = 0.45).
*Caveat:* the HIBI enrichment tables were saved already filtered to padj < 0.05,
so the term universe is under-estimated and this fold-enrichment is an upper
bound.

**Direction — the strongest hint toward reversal found anywhere in this work.**
51 of 85 shared pathways (**60%**) move in opposite directions, binomial
*p* = 0.082. The top synaptic pathways are uniformly HIBI-down / GLP-1R-up:

| Pathway | HIBI score | GLP-1R score |
|---|---|---|
| Neuronal System | −1.29 | +0.06 |
| Neuron Projection | −1.15 | +0.12 |
| Postsynaptic Density | −1.15 | +0.04 |
| Dendrite | −1.13 | +0.09 |
| Asymmetric Synapse | −1.06 | +0.06 |
| Axon guidance | −0.73 | +0.25 |

**But read the magnitudes before reading the signs.** Every GLP-1R score above is
between +0.04 and +0.25 — hovering just above zero — while the HIBI scores are
around −1.1. The "opposite direction" call rests entirely on the sign of a very
small number, exactly the fragility `Fig12F` documents. Consistent with that, the
magnitude-weighted permutation test is not significant (*z* = −1.01) and the
correlation between pathway scores is weakly positive (*r* = +0.11, *p* = 0.32).
So: a real trend at 60%, worth reporting as such, but not significant and resting
on a compressed GLP-1R signal.

**Same pathways, different genes.** Median gene-membership Jaccard across shared
pathways is **0.19**. The two conditions largely engage the same pathways through
*different* member genes. Shared enrichment therefore does not imply a shared
molecular route, which tempers how much the overlap in `Fig12C` can be claimed to
mean.

### Effect-size quality control

| File (in `figures/`) | Content | Generated by |
|---|---|---|
| `Fig12F_EffectSize_QC` | A: \|mean log2FC\| distribution per arm. B: why the GLP-1R arm is compressed — cancellation across its 31 groups. C: effect-size floor sweep showing what happens as near-zero genes are removed. | `code/01_meta_analysis_pipeline/14_effectsize_qc.py` |

Many genes counted as "same direction" have mean log2FC near zero, which makes
their direction call close to meaningless. Quantifying that:

- **The GLP-1R arm is heavily compressed.** Median \|mean log2FC\| is **0.27** vs
  **0.87** for HIBI; **34%** of GLP-1R values fall below the 0.2 DEG threshold,
  against 7% for HIBI.
- **The cause is cancellation.** The GLP-1R consensus averages 31 heterogeneous
  groups (different agonists, brain regions, disease contexts). Genes whose
  groups disagree in direction have median \|mean log2FC\| of **0.08**; genes
  unanimous across their groups reach **0.33**. Averaging destroys the signal for
  the mixed genes. The HIBI arm suffers less — 78% of its genes are unanimous
  across groups, versus 57% for GLP-1R.
- **Direction calls are unreliable there.** For genes below \|LFC\| 0.2, the
  per-group vote and `sign(mean_log2FC)` disagree **32%** of the time; above 0.5
  they disagree only 7%.

Two consequences, and they cut in different directions:

1. **The near-zero genes were inflating the *discordant* count, not the
   concordant one** — 19% of discordant genes sit below \|LFC\| 0.05 versus 10%
   of concordant genes. Removing them does not reveal a reversal: the direction
   split stays near 50% and Pearson *r* stays weakly positive at every threshold
   (see Panel C). Restricting instead to genes unanimous across groups in both
   arms (n = 735) gives 45% opposite, *r* = +0.058, n.s.
2. **But mean log2FC is a poor summary statistic for the GLP-1R arm**, and any
   conclusion resting on it inherits that weakness. A gene can be a robust DEG in
   12 groups and still average to ~0. This attenuates any correlation toward
   zero, so the near-zero correlation should be read as "no signal detectable
   with this summary" rather than "no relationship exists."

For a stronger comparison the GLP-1R side needs a summary that does not cancel —
per-group directional consistency, or a rank/GSEA-based score computed per group
and then combined — rather than a mean fold-change across heterogeneous groups.

### Magnitude-aware version

| File (in `figures/`) | Content | Generated by |
|---|---|---|
| `Fig12E_Magnitude_Opposition` | Panel A: opposition candidates — genes moving \|log2FC\| ≥ 0.5 in **both** arms in opposite directions with ≥3 groups supporting each call, as diverging bars showing real effect sizes. Panel B: the global magnitude-weighted opposition score against a 20,000-draw permutation null. | `code/01_meta_analysis_pipeline/13_magnitude_figure.py` |

Candidate table: `data/meta_analysis/opposition_candidates.csv`.

Direction votes and Spearman correlation are both magnitude-blind — Spearman uses
ranks only. Repeating the comparison with effect size included **strengthens the
concordance rather than the reversal**:

| Magnitude-aware test | Result |
|---|---|
| Pearson *r* (uses magnitude) | **+0.083**, *p* = 6.6e-4 — positive, and larger than Spearman ρ = +0.051 |
| Genes with \|log2FC\| ≥ 0.5 in both arms | 51% opposite (n = 179), *p* = 0.77 |
| Genes with \|log2FC\| ≥ 1.0 in both arms | **34% opposite** (n = 29) — i.e. 66% *same* direction |
| Magnitude-weighted opposition score Σ(−x·y) vs permutation null | observed −73.6 against null +53.7 ± 37.4, **z = −3.40** — significantly *less* opposition than chance |

Requiring larger movement in both arms pushes the result further toward
concordance, and the magnitude-weighted score sits 3.4 SD below its null. So the
lack of a reversal signal is not an artefact of collapsing effect sizes to
UP/DOWN calls.

What magnitude *does* provide is a principled candidate list rather than post-hoc
gene picking: **24 genes** move at least 0.5 log2FC in both arms, in opposite
directions, with ≥3 groups supporting each call. `Fkbp5` (8/12 groups), `Hspa1a`
(8/7), `Plxna4` (8/4), `Cnksr2` (7/6), `Sult1a1` (7/5), `Bmpr2` (6/6) and
`Hspa1b` (6/4) are the best-supported. These are legitimate follow-up targets;
they are simply outweighed by concordant genes genome-wide.

Axis limits are set from the 90th percentile of |log2FC| per panel; a handful of
genes (e.g. `F11r` at +9.9 in the GLP-1R arm) fall outside and are drawn as
hollow triangles at the boundary. Scaling to them would compress every other
gene into the origin.

**Fig12B differs deliberately from panel B of the composite.** In the composite,
the two opposing quadrants were shaded and *only discordant genes were labelled* —
labels were ranked by recurrence among reversals, so every gene name shown was a
reversal by construction. That combination manufactured the appearance of an
antagonistic relationship. In the standalone version the shading is removed,
labels are drawn from the most recurrent shared genes regardless of direction,
both axes share a range, and the measured relationship is printed on the panel.

Supporting tables written by the same script:

- `data/meta_analysis/shared_pathways_GLP1R_HIBI.csv` — 85 pathways significant in both arms, with each arm's adjusted p
- `data/meta_analysis/shared_genes_GLP1R_HIBI.csv` — all 1,715 shared consensus genes, direction and recurrence in each arm, concordant flag

Two analysis choices worth knowing when reading this figure:

- **Direction comes from `predominant_direction`** (the per-group UP/DOWN vote), not from `sign(mean_log2FC)`. The two disagree for roughly 350 genes per arm, because one extreme group can flip the mean. The vote is the robust call and is what the concordant/discordant counts use.
- **Reversals are ranked by recurrence** (groups supporting the call in each arm), not by |log2FC| gap. A handful of genes carry |mean log2FC| > 10 — low-expression/single-group artefacts — and ranking by magnitude surfaces those instead of the real biology. 52 of 1,715 genes are clipped to the Panel B axis limits, and Panel D is clipped at ±2 like every other figure in both arms.

### What the cross-arm comparison does and does not support

The two arms **share 1,715 consensus genes and 85 enriched pathways**, and the
shared pathways are overwhelmingly synaptic and neurodevelopmental. That is a
real observation and is what these figures document.

The comparison does **not** demonstrate that GLP-1R agonism reverses the injury
signature:

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Direction split (840/1,715 opposite) | 49% — binomial vs 50:50, *p* = 0.41 (chance) |
| Restricted to genes in ≥4 / ≥5 / ≥6 groups in both arms | 46% / 39% / 46% opposite, all n.s. |
| Spearman correlation of mean log2FC across shared genes | ρ = **+0.055** — near zero and slightly *positive* |
| Genes strongly HI-induced (LFC > +1) | mean GLP-1R log2FC = **+0.31** (also up, not down) |
| HI-up vs HI-down genes, GLP-1R response | HI-up genes score *higher*, Mann-Whitney *p* = 0.016 (concordant) |
| Gene-set overlap enrichment | depends on assumed background: 1.01× (n.s.) at a 15,000-gene universe, 1.35× at 20,000 |

A systematic reversal effect would produce a clearly negative correlation. It
does not. The underlying reason is a design limitation rather than an analysis
one: the GLP-1R datasets are obesity, aging, neurodegeneration and TBI models —
none is neonatal hypoxic-ischemic injury — so the two arms are independent
meta-analyses of different conditions, and reversal cannot be inferred from
comparing them.

Individual high-recurrence genes such as `Fkbp5` (down in 8 HIBI groups, up in
12 GLP-1R groups) remain worthwhile candidates, but they are selected post hoc
from ~1,700 genes and should be treated as hypotheses. The defensible framing is
convergence on shared synaptic programs and candidate-gene generation, not
demonstrated signature reversal.

## Method notes

**Consensus Cnet** (`06f`) — genes ranked purely by recurrence (`n_groups`),
*not* by log2FC and *not* by enrichment strength; this is the same gene ranking
GLP-1R uses for its consensus heatmap and GOChord. All 30 top-frequent genes are
forced into the graph as nodes even if they land in no enriched term, so the
figure always shows the full signature. Terms are picked by greedy set-cover over
those genes (max 12).

**Restricted / focused Cnets** (`06d`) — terms come from enriching *only* that
pathway's HIBI consensus genes (the way the GOplot chord does), rather than
keyword-filtering a whole-consensus enrichment. Terms are then taken in
adjusted-p order, dropping any whose pathway-gene overlap has Jaccard ≥ 0.8 with
an already-selected term, so the network stays readable. Max 12 terms, max 20
genes per term edge list.

Both use the unmodified `cnet_style.py` and `cnet_gene_lists.py` copied from the
GLP-1R project, so styling, colours, node sizing, spring layout (k=3.5, 150
iterations, seed=42) and library colour legend are identical between the two
projects. Enrichment via Enrichr (gseapy), mouse organism, across
GO-BP/MF/CC + KEGG_2021_Human + Reactome_2022.
